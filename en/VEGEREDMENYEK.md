### The Center of Mass Theorem
- **Exercise 1:** Following the explosion, the common center of mass of the flying fragments continues to move **along exactly the same parabolic trajectory** that the rocket would have followed without the explosion. Since the explosion is an internal force, the gravity remains the only external force acting on the particle system.
- **Exercise 2:** The acceleration of the system's center of mass during and after the push is **$0\text{ m/s}^2$** (it remains at rest). The push is an internal interaction; in the absence of an external force, the state of motion of the center of mass cannot change.
- **Exercise 3:** 
  * Acceleration of the center of mass: **$2\text{ m/s}^2$** (since $a = \frac{F}{m_1 + m_2} = \frac{10\text{ N}}{5\text{ kg}}$).
  * The role of internal force: The internal tension force **does not matter at all** regarding the acceleration of the center of mass, because the resultant of internal forces is always zero due to Newton's Third Law (action-reaction). Only the external force $F$ determines the acceleration.

### The Atwood Machine
- **Exercise 1:** 
  * Acceleration based on masses: $a = \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2} \cdot g$
  * Acceleration based on displacement ($s$) and time ($t$): $a = \frac{2s}{t^2}$
  * Comparison: Factoring in measurement and frictional losses, these two values match identically.
- **Exercise 2:** 
  * Initial total mechanical energy at launch ($v=0$, the heavier body is at height $h$): $E_{\text{start}} = m_1 g h$
  * Final total mechanical energy right before impact ($h=0$, both bodies move with velocity $v$): $E_{\text{final}} = m_2 g h + \frac{1}{2}(m_1 + m_2)v^2$
  * Proof of conservation: Since $v^2 = 2ah = 2 \left(\frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2} g\right) h$, substituting this yields $E_{\text{start}} = E_{\text{final}}$, proving that mechanical energy is completely conserved.
- **Exercise 3:** 
  * Descent of the center of mass: $\Delta y_{\text{COM}} = \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2} \cdot s$
  * Time of change: $t = \sqrt{\frac{2s}{a}}$ (identical to the transit time of the bodies)
  * Acceleration of the center of mass from kinematics: $a_{\text{COM}} = \frac{2 \cdot \Delta y_{\text{COM}}}{t^2} = \left(\frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\right)^2 \cdot g$
  * Verification from the Center of Mass Theorem: Since the net external force is $F_{\text{external}} = (m_1 - m_2)g$, and the internal tension cancels itself out, the theorem yields $a_{\text{COM}} = \frac{F_{\text{external}}}{m_1 + m_2} \cdot \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2} = \left(\frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\right)^2 \cdot g$, which matches identically.
