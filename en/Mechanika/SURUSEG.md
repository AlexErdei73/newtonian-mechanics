# Density

## Practicing Units

### Examples with Units of Area

1. The area of a corridor is $5.8\text{ m}^2$. What is the minimum number of tiles needed to cover it if each tile is a rectangle measuring $15\text{ cm} \cdot 21\text{ cm}$?

It is simpler to work in units of $\text{cm}^2$ since the dimensions of the tile are given in this unit.

$$
A = 5.8\text{ m}^2 = 5.8 \cdot 100\text{ cm} \cdot 100\text{ cm} = 5.8 \cdot 10^4\text{ cm}^2
$$

$$
A_{\text{lap}} = 15\text{ cm} \cdot 21\text{ cm} = 315\text{ cm}^2
$$

$$
N = \frac {A} {A_{\text{lap}}} = \frac {5.8 \cdot 10^4\text{ cm}^2} {315\text{ cm}^2} \approx 184.1
$$

This means that a minimum of $185\text{ tiles}$ is required to pave the corridor.

2. The area of a forest is $15\text{ km}^2$. How many tree saplings are required to replant the forest if a spacing of $1.5\text{ m}$ is maintained between the trees, considering both rows and columns? The edges can be neglected.

Here it is practical to calculate in $\text{m}^2$.

$$
A = 15\text{ km}^2 = 15 \cdot 1000\text{ m} \cdot 1000\text{ m} = 15 \cdot 10^6\text{ m}^2
$$

$$
A_{\text{fa}} = 1.5\text{ m} \cdot 1.5\text{ m} = 2.25\text{ m}^2
$$

$$
N = \frac {A} {A_{\text{fa}}} = \frac {15 \cdot 10^6\text{ m}^2} {2.25\text{ m}^2} \approx 6,670,000
$$

Therefore, $6,670,000\text{ tree saplings}$ are required for the reforestation. Here we rounded to three significant figures because our result cannot be more precise than the initial data, which was given to two significant figures (we can retain at most one additional significant figure during calculations).

### Examples with Units of Volume

1. How many $5\text{ ml}$ syringes can be filled from a $1.5\text{ l}$ bottle of physiological saline solution? How many $\text{cm}^3$ is the volume of the syringe? How many $\text{m}^3$ is the volume of the saline solution?

$$
V = 1.5\text{ l} = 1.5\text{ dm}^3 = 1.5 \cdot 10\text{ cm} \cdot 10\text{ cm} \cdot 10\text{ cm} = 1500\text{ cm}^3 = 1500\text{ ml}
$$

From this it is clearly visible that $1\text{ cm}^3 = 1\text{ ml}$.

$$
V_{\text{fecskendő}} = 5\text{ ml} = 5\text{ cm}^3
$$

$$
N = \frac {V} {V_{\text{fecskendő}}} = \frac {1500\text{ ml}} {5\text{ ml}} = 300
$$

$$
V = 1.5\text{ l} = 1.5\text{ dm}^3 = 1.5 \cdot 0.1\text{ m} \cdot 0.1\text{ m} \cdot 0.1\text{ m} = 0.0015\text{ m}^3
$$

2. The tank of a tanker is a cylinder with a length of $6\text{ m}$ and a radius of $1.5\text{ m}$. How many liters of fuel can fit into it if we only fill it to $70\%$ capacity?

$$
V_{\text{tanker}} = r^2 \cdot \pi \cdot h = (1.5\text{ m})^2 \cdot \pi \cdot 6\text{ m} \approx 42.41\text{ m}^3
$$

$$
V = 0.70 \cdot V_{\text{tanker}} = 0.7 \cdot 42.41\text{ m}^3 \approx 29.69\text{ m}^3
$$

$$
V = 29.69\text{ m}^3 = 29.69 \cdot 10\text{ dm} \cdot 10\text{ dm} \cdot 10\text{ dm} = 29,690\text{ dm}^3 = 29,690\text{ l}
$$

The initial data are given to two significant figures, so the final result must not be reported to more than three significant figures. The correct answer is therefore: there are $29,700\text{ l}$ of fuel in the tanker.

## Density

> **The ratio of a body's mass to its volume is called the density of the body. Density is a quantity characteristic of the material quality. The symbol for density is the Greek letter $\rho$ (rho), and its fundamental SI unit of measurement is $\frac {\text{kg}} {\text{m}^3}$.**

$$
\rho = \frac {m} {V}
$$

### Examples with Density

1. The mass of the fuel in the tanker is $21,600\text{ kg}$. We calculated the volume of the fuel, which is $29.7\text{ m}^3$. What is the density of the fuel?

$$
\rho = \frac{m}{V} = \frac{21,600\text{ kg}}{29.7\text{ m}^3} \approx 727\text{ kg/m}^3
$$

2. A beaker was filled with $500\text{ g}$ of mercury. How high will the mercury stand in it if the inner radius of the beaker is $1.5\text{ cm}$? The density of mercury is $13,600\text{ kg/m}^3$.

$$
V = \frac{m}{\rho} = \frac{0.5\text{ kg}}{13,600\text{ kg/m}^3} \approx 3.68 \cdot 10^{-5}\text{ m}^3
$$

$$
A = \pi \cdot r^2 = \pi \cdot (0.015\text{ m})^2 \approx 7.07 \cdot 10^{-4}\text{ m}^2
$$

$$
h = \frac{V}{A} = \frac{3.68 \cdot 10^{-5}\text{ m}^3}{7.07 \cdot 10^{-4}\text{ m}^2} \approx 0.052\text{ m} = 5.2\text{ cm}
$$

3. Calculate the mass of $1\text{ cm}^3$ of mercury! How many $\frac {\text{kg}} {\text{m}^3}$ is $1\text{ }\frac {\text{g}} {\text{cm}^3}$?

The density of mercury is $13,600\text{ }\frac {\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$. Let us convert the density into units of $\frac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3}$:

$$
\rho = 13,600\text{ }\frac {\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3} = 13,600 \cdot \frac {1000\text{ g}} {100\text{ cm} \cdot 100\text{ cm} \cdot 100\text{ cm}} = 13,600 \cdot \frac{1000\text{ g}}{1,000,000\text{ cm}^3} = 13.6\text{ }\frac {\text{g}} {\text{cm}^3}
$$

From this, the sought mass is:

$$
m = \rho \cdot V = 13.6\text{ }\frac {\text{g}} {\text{cm}^3} \cdot 1\text{ cm}^3 = 13.6\text{ g}
$$

From this it follows that $1\text{ }\frac {\text{g}} {\text{cm}^3}$ is exactly equal to $1000\text{ }\frac {\text{kg}} {\text{m}^3}$.

## Exercises

1. The floor area of a room is $12\text{ m}^2$. What is the minimum number of $30\text{ cm} \cdot 30\text{ cm}$ tiles needed to cover it?
2. The area of a garden is $2.5\text{ km}^2$. How many bushes can be planted here if a spacing of $2\text{ m}$ is left between the bushes in all directions? The edges are neglected.
3. Into how many $10\text{ ml}$ glasses can the contents of a $2\text{ liter}$ bottle be poured? How many $\text{cm}^3$ is the volume of one glass? How many $\text{dm}^3$ is that of the bottle?
4. A cylindrical container is $4\text{ m}$ high and has a radius of $2\text{ m}$. How many liters of water can fit into it if we only fill it to $80\%$ of its volume?
5. The mass of an aluminum rod is $1500\text{ kg}$, and its volume is $0.5\text{ m}^3$. What is the density of the aluminum?
6. The mass of a bottle of water is $1.2\text{ kg}$, and its volume is $1.2\text{ dm}^3$. What is the density of the water?
7. The edge length of an aluminum cube is $10\text{ cm}$, and its mass is $2.7\text{ kg}$. What is the density of the aluminum?
8. The volume of a helium balloon is $5\text{ m}^3$, and the mass of the gas inside it is $10\text{ kg}$ (the mass of the envelope is negligible). What is the density of the helium in this state?
9. We pour $300\text{ g}$ of oil into a beaker. How high will the oil stand if the inner radius of the beaker is $5\text{ cm}$? The density of the oil is $900\text{ kg/m}^3$.
10. Calculate the mass of $1\text{ dm}^3$ of water if the density of water is $1000\text{ kg/m}^3$! How many grams is this mass?
