Velocity

The Concept of Rectilinear Uniform Motion

When an object moves along a straight path with a constant magnitude and direction of velocity, we speak of rectilinear uniform motion.

Example: A car travels uniformly at a speed of 60 km/h60\text{ km/h}. What does this mean in practice? A speed of 60 km/h60\text{ km/h} means that the car covers a distance of 60 km60\text{ km} per hour. Since one hour consists of 6060 minutes, the car moves forward by exactly 1 km1\text{ km} per minute.

During uniform motion, the object covers equal distances in equal time intervals. We can also say that there is a direct proportionality between the distance traveled and the elapsed time.

In the case of rectilinear uniform motion, the object moves along a straight path such that it always covers equal distances in arbitrarily chosen, equal time intervals.

The Concept and Formula of Velocity

Velocity represents the distance traveled per unit of time; its value is constant in the case of uniform motion. Velocity is actually a vector quantity: its direction coincides with the instantaneous direction of motion. In uniform motion, the magnitude of the velocity remains strictly constant, and in rectilinear motion, the direction of the velocity (v→\vec{v}) also remains strictly constant.

Velocity represents the quotient of the distance traveled and the time required to cover it in the case of uniform motion. Symbol: vv, fundamental SI unit: m/s\text{m/s}.

v=st v = \frac{s}{t}

In this formula, ss denotes the distance traveled, and tt denotes the corresponding time duration.

Examples

  1. A car travels uniformly, its speedometer shows 60 km/h60\text{ km/h}. What is this velocity in units of m/s\text{m/s}? What distance does the car cover in 20 s20\text{ s}? How much time does it take to cover 100 m100\text{ m}?

Velocity conversion:

v=st=60 km1 h=60000 m3600 s≈16.7 ms v = \frac{s}{t} = \frac{60\text{ km}}{1\text{ h}} = \frac{60\ 000\text{ m}}{3600\text{ s}} \approx 16.7\text{ }\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}

(Since the initial data was given to two significant figures, we can retain three significant figures in the intermediate calculation, but we must pay attention to precision in the final practical answers.)

Let us find the distance traveled in 20 s20\text{ s} (let this be the unknown xx):

16.7 ms=x20 s 16.7\text{ }\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} = \frac{x}{20\text{ s}}

Multiplying both sides of the equation by 20 s20\text{ s}, we obtain:

x=16.7 ms⋅20 s=334 m x = 16.7\text{ }\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} \cdot 20\text{ s} = 334\text{ m}

Based on the precision of the initial data, the last digit is already uncertain, so in reality, the car covers approximately 330 m330\text{ m} when rounded.

Let us find the time required to cover 100 m100\text{ m}, where the unknown is also denoted by xx:

16.7 ms=100 mx 16.7\text{ }\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} = \frac{100\text{ m}}{x}

To simplify the calculation, we can omit the units during rearrangement, since the time will automatically be obtained in seconds:

16.7⋅x=100 16.7 \cdot x = 100

x=10016.7≈6.0 s x = \frac{100}{16.7} \approx 6.0\text{ s}

Thus, the car covers the 100 m100\text{ m} distance in exactly 6 s6\text{ s}.

  1. A car travels uniformly at a speed of 30 ms30\text{ }\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}. What distance does it cover in 3600 s3600\text{ s} (meaning one hour)? What is its speed in km/h\text{km/h}? How much time does it take to reach a city located at a distance of 150 km150\text{ km}?

Let us calculate the distance traveled in one hour (xx):

30=x3600 30 = \frac{x}{3600}

x=30⋅3600=108000 m=108 km x = 30 \cdot 3600 = 108\ 000\text{ m} = 108\text{ km}

Since the car covers 108 km108\text{ km} in a single hour, its velocity can be directly written in the form of 108 km/h108\text{ km/h}.

Now let us determine the time required for the 150 km150\text{ km} distance (xx), already using the calculated km/h\text{km/h} unit:

108=150x 108 = \frac{150}{x}

108⋅x=150 108 \cdot x = 150

x=150108≈1.39 h x = \frac{150}{108} \approx 1.39\text{ h}

Since 0.39 h=0.39⋅60 min≈23 min0.39\text{ h} = 0.39 \cdot 60\text{ min} \approx 23\text{ min}, the travel time is 1 hour 23 minutes1\text{ hour } 23\text{ minutes}.
