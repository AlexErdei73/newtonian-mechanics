# Pressure

## The Concept and Calculation of Pressure

### Experiments

[Demonstrating the surface area dependence of pressure with ice cream cones](https://youtube.com)

[A person and a balloon on a bed of nails](https://youtube.com)

[The depth of an impression left in dough shows how pressure depends on surface area](https://youtube.com)

During the study of friction phenomena, we observed that the friction force also depended on the force compressing the contacting surfaces. However, the constraint force perpendicular to the surface, known as the normal force (or pressing force), can also cause deformation—for instance, leaving a footprint in flour or dough, or popping a balloon. In our experiments, we saw that the extent of deformation depends not only on the magnitude of the force but also on the size of the surface area over which this force acts. In physics, the deformation effect of a force can be characterized by introducing a new quantity: pressure.

> **Pressure is defined as the ratio of the normal force acting perpendicular to a surface to the area of that surface. The symbol for pressure is p, and its SI unit is the Pascal (Pa).**

$$
p = \frac{F}{A}
$$

$$
1\text{ Pa} = \frac{1\text{ N}}{1\text{ m}^2}
$$

The role of the surface area is excellently illustrated by the bed of nails experiment. If we step onto a single nail, our entire body weight is concentrated onto a negligibly small surface area ($A$), which generates an enormous pressure, causing the nail to instantly pierce right through the shoe. If, however, we lie down on a bed consisting of 500 nails, the pressing force (weight) is evenly distributed across a large surface area. Consequently, the pressure exerted on each individual nail is drastically reduced, leaving the balloon or the human body completely unharmed.

### Examples

1. A rectangular block with a mass of $200\text{ g}$ has edges of $8\text{ cm}$, $5\text{ cm}$, and $2\text{ cm}$. What pressure does the object exert at rest on a horizontal tabletop if the acceleration due to gravity is $9.81\text{ m/s}^2$?

$$
F = F_g = mg = 0.2 \cdot 9.81 = 1.962\text{ N}
$$

The three different contact surface areas (faces):

$$
A_1 = ab = 0.08 \cdot 0.05 = 4 \cdot 10^{-3}\text{ m}^2
$$

$$
A_2 = bc = 0.05 \cdot 0.02 = 10^{-3}\text{ m}^2
$$

$$
A_3 = ac = 0.08 \cdot 0.02 = 1.6 \cdot 10^{-3}\text{ m}^2
$$

The pressures exerted in each respective case:

$$
p_1 = \frac{F}{A_1} = \frac{1.962}{4 \cdot 10^{-3}} = 490.5\text{ Pa}
$$

$$
p_2 = \frac{F}{A_2} = \frac{1.962}{10^{-3}} = 1,962\text{ Pa}
$$

$$
p_3 = \frac{F}{A_3} = \frac{1.962}{1.6 \cdot 10^{-3}} = 1,226.25\text{ Pa}
$$

It can be seen that the block leaves the deepest impression when contacting the surface with its smallest area face ($A_2$), because it exerts the greatest pressure in this configuration.

2. A balloon is pressed onto the tip of a nail with a diameter of $0.1\text{ mm}$ with just enough force to make it burst. At this moment, the pressure is $38.20\text{ MPa}$. What force is exerted at the instant of bursting? What will the pressure be if the balloon is pressed with the exact same force onto a bed consisting of 100 identical nails?

$$
p = \frac{F}{A}
$$

The circular cross-sectional area of a single nail tip ($d = 0.1\text{ mm} \rightarrow R = 0.05\text{ mm} = 0.05 \cdot 10^{-3}\text{ m}$):

$$
A = R^2 \pi = (0.05 \cdot 10^{-3})^2 \cdot 3.14159 \approx 7.854 \cdot 10^{-9}\text{ m}^2
$$

The exerted force:

$$
F = pA = 38.20 \cdot 10^6 \cdot 7.854 \cdot 10^{-9} \approx 0.300\text{ N}
$$

If we press the balloon onto 100 identical nails, the surface area increases a hundredfold ($A_{\text{new}} = 100A$), so the pressure drops to one-hundredth of its original value:

$$
p_{\text{new}} = \frac{F}{100A} = \frac{38.20 \cdot 10^6}{100} = 382,000\text{ Pa} = 382\text{ kPa}
$$

## Atmospheric Pressure

### Experiments

[The Magdeburg hemispheres](https://youtube.com)

[Air pressure crushes a soda can](https://youtube.com)

[Air pressure crushes a steel drum](https://youtube.com)

These experiments demonstrate that the surrounding air continuously exerts an extremely high pressure on its environment. This pressure remains virtually unnoticed as long as the internal pressure of objects balances it out. However, as soon as this equilibrium is disrupted—either because we evacuate the air from inside the hemispheres, or because heated water vapor rapidly condenses upon cooling, leading to a dramatic drop in internal pressure—the immense external atmospheric pressure and the resulting net force can crush steel drums or aluminum cans with brutal force.

> **The standard atmospheric pressure value is $101,325\text{ Pa} = 1\text{ atm}$. Another unit similar to the atmosphere and frequently used in engineering is the bar: $1\text{ bar} = 10^5\text{ Pa}$. The nominal value of atmospheric pressure is referenced to sea level. Atmospheric pressure decreases rapidly with altitude, since the thickness of the air layer above us that weighs down on us becomes increasingly thinner. Weather fronts also significantly influence the local value of atmospheric pressure.**

### Example

1. How many $\text{N}$ of force are required to pull apart the Magdeburg hemispheres of radius $5\text{ cm}$ if the air inside them has been completely evacuated? Let the external atmospheric pressure be exactly $1.01 \cdot 10^5\text{ Pa}$!

Atmospheric pressure acts perpendicularly from all directions onto the spherical surface, but when calculating the force needed to pull them apart, the relevant area is the cross-sectional plane perpendicular to the direction of pulling (the area of the circular disk where they mate):

$$
A = R^2 \pi = 0.05^2 \cdot 3.14159 \approx 7.854 \cdot 10^{-3}\text{ m}^2
$$

The force compressing the hemispheres together:

$$
F = pA = 1.01 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 7.854 \cdot 10^{-3} \approx 793.25\text{ N}
$$

This force corresponds to the weight of a mass of approximately $81\text{ kg}$, which explains why it is impossible to pull them apart with bare hands without breaking the vacuum.

## Problems

1. **High-heeled shoe pressure:** A woman with a mass of $55\text{ kg}$ is wearing high-heeled shoes, where the heel can be modeled as a square with a surface area of $1\text{ cm}^2$. At a certain moment while walking, her entire weight rests on a single shoe heel.
    * a) Calculate the pressure exerted by the heel on the ground at this moment!
    * b) How many times greater is this pressure than standard atmospheric pressure ($1\text{ atm}$)? (Use the value $g = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$!)

2. **Force acting on a diver:** A scuba diver is swimming in the sea, where the combined pressure of the water and the atmosphere at a certain depth is $3.5\text{ bar}$. The effective surface area of the diver's back is approximately $0.45\text{ m}^2$. What is the total compressive force acting on the diver's back at this depth?

3. **Evacuated glass container:** The lid of a rectangular, rigid-walled glass container is $15\text{ cm}$ long and $10\text{ cm}$ wide. The air is evacuated from the container, making the internal pressure negligible compared to the external atmospheric pressure ($101,325\text{ Pa}$). What vertical force must we apply to lift the lid and open the container, assuming the mass of the lid and the adhesion of the seal are negligible?

4. **Military tracked vehicle:** An $18\text{ -tonne}$ tracked vehicle crosses soft, muddy ground. The total surface area of the vehicle's two tracks in contact with the ground is $4.5\text{ m}^2$ combined.
    * a) What pressure does the vehicle exert on the ground?
    * b) Why is using tracks more advantageous than conventional wheels on loose ground? Formulate your answer based on the pressure formula!

5. **Load on a window pane:** A modern window pane in an office building is $2.5\text{ m}$ high and $1.8\text{ m}$ wide. Due to a fault in the interior air conditioning system, a pressure of $100,500\text{ Pa}$ acts on one side of the window, while the standard atmospheric air pressure ($101,325\text{ Pa}$) pushes against the glass on the outside.
    * a) What is the magnitude and direction of the net force acting on the window pane due to the pressure difference?
    * b) Why do windows not break under normal circumstances despite the immense atmospheric pressure acting on them?
