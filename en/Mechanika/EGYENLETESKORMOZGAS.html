Uniform Circular Motion

When an object moves at a constant speed along a circular path, it is undergoing uniform circular motion. We have seen that for uniform motion, the magnitude of the velocity is constant. In uniform circular motion, however, the direction of the velocity changes from moment to moment, always pointing in the direction of the tangent to the circle, making it perpendicular to the radius. This is because over any short time interval, the direction of the displacement vector of the object is perpendicular to the radius. This becomes increasingly true the shorter the time interval we examine. The displacement vector, in turn, is parallel to the instantaneous velocity.

Experiment

The experiment also demonstrates that the velocity of uniform circular motion is always tangential, and therefore perpendicular to the radius of the circle.

The velocity of circular motion points in the direction of the tangent to the circle, meaning it is perpendicular to the radius.

Simulation

Uniform circular motion

Period

The time required to complete one full revolution is the period. Symbol: TT

Calculating the velocity:

v=st v = \frac{s}{t}

The circumference of a circle:

s=2πR s = 2\pi R

Therefore:

v=2πRT v = \frac{2\pi R}{T}

Angular Velocity

The angle of rotation per unit time is the angular velocity. Symbol: ω\omega, unit: rads\frac{\text{rad}}{\text{s}}

ω=ϕt \omega = \frac{\phi}{t}

The angle of rotation is measured in radians. A complete revolution, which is 360∘360^\circ, is 2π2\pi radians. A straight angle (180∘180^\circ) is π\pi radians. If the direction of rotation is positive—meaning counter-clockwise—then the angle of rotation is also positive. For a negative direction of rotation—clockwise—the angle of rotation is negative. Angular velocity is also a signed quantity. It is positive for a positive direction of rotation and negative for a negative direction of rotation.

ω=2πT \omega = \frac{2\pi}{T}

v=ωR v = \omega R

Example

The motion of the Earth during its orbit around the Sun can be considered a uniform circular motion to a good approximation. The radius of the circle is 149.6⋅106 km149.6 \cdot 10^6\text{ km}, and the period is 365.26 days365.26\text{ days}. What is the orbital velocity? What is the angular velocity?

v=2πRT=2π⋅149.6⋅109 m365.26⋅86400 s≈29784 m/s v = \frac{2\pi R}{T} = \frac{2\pi \cdot 149.6 \cdot 10^9\text{ m}}{365.26 \cdot 86400\text{ s}} \approx 29784\text{ m/s}

ω=2πT=2π365.26⋅86400 s≈1.99⋅10−7 rad/s \omega = \frac{2\pi}{T} = \frac{2\pi}{365.26 \cdot 86400\text{ s}} \approx 1.99 \cdot 10^{-7}\text{ rad/s}

Exercises

  1. The drum of a washing machine rotates 1200 times per minute during spinning (1200 rpm1200\text{ rpm}). What is the angular velocity of the drum expressed in radians/s?
  2. What is the linear velocity of the tip of a wall clock’s minute hand, which is 15 cm15\text{ cm} long? (Be careful to select the correct period!)
  3. A car travels at a constant speed of 72 km/h72\text{ km/h} through a roundabout. The radius of the roundabout is 40 meters40\text{ meters}. How much time does it take for the car to complete one full loop (what is the period)?
  4. The length (radius) of a helicopter’s rotor blade is 5 meters5\text{ meters}. The angular velocity of the rotor is 40 rad/s40\text{ rad/s}. What is the speed at the very tip of the blade?
  5. At the equator, the Earth has a radius of approximately 6370 km6370\text{ km} and completes one revolution around its axis in 24 hours24\text{ hours}. Calculate the linear velocity of a point on the equator!
