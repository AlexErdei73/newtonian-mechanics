# Pascal's Law

## Properties of Gases and Liquids

### Experiment

[The operation of hydraulic machines](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sgUMV7EtCkE)

The experiment demonstrates that both liquids and gases take the shape of their container; thus, unlike solid objects, they do not possess a fixed shape.

> **Liquids and gases do not have a fixed shape; they take the shape of their container.**

Furthermore, it can be established that fluids (liquids and gases) at rest exert only compressive forces—meaning forces perpendicular to the surface—on objects submerged in them or on the walls of their container. The force component parallel to the surfaces remains zero as long as the fluid is in equilibrium.

> **Inside fluids at rest, only forces perpendicular to the surfaces arise. This contrasts with solid objects, in which forces parallel to the surfaces can occur even in an equilibrium state.**

A major difference, however, is that while the volume of liquids cannot be significantly reduced even by applying immense pressures—making liquids practically incompressible—gases fully occupy the volume of any closed container and are highly compressible.

> **Unlike gases, liquids are incompressible. This means that liquids possess a constant volume, whereas gases completely fill a closed container, taking its volume.**

## Pascal's Principle

### Experiment

[Pascal's syringe](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZHL2OXGJm00)

The experiment shows that the pressure applied to the fluid at the piston propagates through the fluid in all directions, causing the fluid to squirt out of the small holes equally in every direction.

> **Pascal's Principle: Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid at rest is transmitted undiminished in all directions.**

## The Operation of the Hydraulic Press

### Examples

1. The smaller piston of a hydraulic press has a radius of $1\text{ cm}$. The piston is in equilibrium, maintained by an applied compressive force of $100\text{ N}$. The larger piston has a radius of $10\text{ cm}$.
* What is the surface area of the smaller piston?
* What is the pressure within the system?
* What is the surface area of the larger piston?
* What is the compressive force acting on the larger piston in equilibrium?

$$
A_1 = r_1^2 \cdot \pi = 0.01^2 \cdot 3.1415 = 3.1415 \cdot 10^{-4}\text{ m}^2
$$

$$
p_1 = \frac{F_1}{A_1} = \frac{100}{3.1415 \cdot 10^{-4}} = 318,310\text{ Pa}
$$

According to Pascal's principle, the exact same pressure acts on the larger piston. Because its surface area is much larger, the resulting compressive force is also proportionally much greater.

$$
p_2 = p_1 = 318,310\text{ Pa}
$$

$$
A_2 = r_2^2 \cdot \pi = 0.1^2 \cdot 3.1415 = 3.1415 \cdot 10^{-2}\text{ m}^2
$$

$$
F_2 = p_2 \cdot A_2 = 318,310 \cdot 3.1415 \cdot 10^{-2} = 10,000\text{ N}
$$

2. In the previous example, the smaller piston moves very slowly by $10\text{ cm}$. Equilibrium is maintained throughout the process. The apparatus lifts a load with a weight of $10,000\text{ N}$ very slowly and uniformly.
* How much work was performed during the lift?
* How much does the larger piston rise?
* By how much does the potential energy of the lifted load increase?

$$
W = F_1 \cdot s_1 = 100 \cdot 0.1 = 10\text{ J}
$$

The liquid within the hydraulic system is incompressible, meaning its volume does not change.

$$
\Delta V_1 = -A_1 \cdot s_1 = 3.1415 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 0.1 = -3.1415 \cdot 10^{-5}\text{ m}^3
$$

$$
\Delta V = \Delta V_1 + \Delta V_2 = 0
$$

$$
\Delta V_2 = 3.1415 \cdot 10^{-5}\text{ m}^3
$$

The lifting height:

$$
h = s_2 = \frac{\Delta V_2}{A_2} = \frac{3.1415 \cdot 10^{-5}}{3.1415 \cdot 10^{-2}} = 0.001\text{ m}
$$

The increase in gravitational potential energy:

$$
\Delta E_h = G \cdot h = 10,000 \cdot 0.001 = 10\text{ J}
$$

Thus, we can see that while a hydraulic machine allows us to multiply force, we cannot gain energy, since the potential energy increases by exactly the same amount as the work put into the system.
