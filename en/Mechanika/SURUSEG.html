Practicing Units

Examples with Units of Area

  1. The area of a corridor is 5.8 m25.8\text{ m}^2. What is the minimum number of tiles needed to cover it if each tile is a rectangle measuring 15 cm⋅21 cm15\text{ cm} \cdot 21\text{ cm}?

It is simpler to work in units of cm2\text{cm}^2 since the dimensions of the tile are given in this unit.

A=5.8 m2=5.8⋅100 cm⋅100 cm=5.8⋅104 cm2 A = 5.8\text{ m}^2 = 5.8 \cdot 100\text{ cm} \cdot 100\text{ cm} = 5.8 \cdot 10^4\text{ cm}^2

Atile=15 cm⋅21 cm=315 cm2 A_{\text{tile}} = 15\text{ cm} \cdot 21\text{ cm} = 315\text{ cm}^2

N=AAtile=5.8⋅104 cm2315 cm2≈184.1 N = \frac {A} {A_{\text{tile}}} = \frac {5.8 \cdot 10^4\text{ cm}^2} {315\text{ cm}^2} \approx 184.1

This means that a minimum of 185 tiles185\text{ tiles} is required to pave the corridor.

  1. The area of a forest is 15 km215\text{ km}^2. How many tree saplings are required to replant the forest if a spacing of 1.5 m1.5\text{ m} is maintained between the trees, considering both rows and columns? The edges can be neglected.

Here it is practical to calculate in m2\text{m}^2.

A=15 km2=15⋅1000 m⋅1000 m=15⋅106 m2 A = 15\text{ km}^2 = 15 \cdot 1000\text{ m} \cdot 1000\text{ m} = 15 \cdot 10^6\text{ m}^2

Atree=1.5 m⋅1.5 m=2.25 m2 A_{\text{tree}} = 1.5\text{ m} \cdot 1.5\text{ m} = 2.25\text{ m}^2

N=AAtree=15⋅106 m22.25 m2≈6,670,000 N = \frac {A} {A_{\text{tree}}} = \frac {15 \cdot 10^6\text{ m}^2} {2.25\text{ m}^2} \approx 6,670,000

Therefore, 6,670,000 tree saplings6,670,000\text{ tree saplings} are required for the reforestation. Here we rounded to three significant figures because our result cannot be more precise than the initial data, which was given to two significant figures (we can retain at most one additional significant figure during calculations).

Examples with Units of Volume

  1. How many 5 ml5\text{ ml} syringes can be filled from a 1.5 l1.5\text{ l} bottle of physiological saline solution? How many cm3\text{cm}^3 is the volume of the syringe? How many m3\text{m}^3 is the volume of the saline solution?

V=1.5 l=1.5 dm3=1.5⋅10 cm⋅10 cm⋅10 cm=1500 cm3=1500 ml V = 1.5\text{ l} = 1.5\text{ dm}^3 = 1.5 \cdot 10\text{ cm} \cdot 10\text{ cm} \cdot 10\text{ cm} = 1500\text{ cm}^3 = 1500\text{ ml}

From this it is clearly visible that 1 cm3=1 ml1\text{ cm}^3 = 1\text{ ml}.

Vsyringe=5 ml=5 cm3 V_{\text{syringe}} = 5\text{ ml} = 5\text{ cm}^3

N=VVsyringe=1500 ml5 ml=300 N = \frac {V} {V_{\text{syringe}}} = \frac {1500\text{ ml}} {5\text{ ml}} = 300

V=1.5 l=1.5 dm3=1.5⋅0.1 m⋅0.1 m⋅0.1 m=0.0015 m3 V = 1.5\text{ l} = 1.5\text{ dm}^3 = 1.5 \cdot 0.1\text{ m} \cdot 0.1\text{ m} \cdot 0.1\text{ m} = 0.0015\text{ m}^3

  1. The tank of a tanker is a cylinder with a length of 6 m6\text{ m} and a radius of 1.5 m1.5\text{ m}. How many liters of fuel can fit into it if we only fill it to 70%70\% capacity?

Vtanker=r2⋅π⋅h=(1.5 m)2⋅π⋅6 m≈42.41 m3 V_{\text{tanker}} = r^2 \cdot \pi \cdot h = (1.5\text{ m})^2 \cdot \pi \cdot 6\text{ m} \approx 42.41\text{ m}^3

V=0.70⋅Vtanker=0.7⋅42.41 m3≈29.69 m3 V = 0.70 \cdot V_{\text{tanker}} = 0.7 \cdot 42.41\text{ m}^3 \approx 29.69\text{ m}^3

V=29.69 m3=29.69⋅10 dm⋅10 dm⋅10 dm=29,690 dm3=29,690 l V = 29.69\text{ m}^3 = 29.69 \cdot 10\text{ dm} \cdot 10\text{ dm} \cdot 10\text{ dm} = 29,690\text{ dm}^3 = 29,690\text{ l}

The initial data are given to two significant figures, so the final result must not be reported to more than three significant figures. The correct answer is therefore: there are 29,700 l29,700\text{ l} of fuel in the tanker.

Density

The ratio of a body’s mass to its volume is called the density of the body. Density is a quantity characteristic of the material quality. The symbol for density is the Greek letter ρ\rho (rho), and its fundamental SI unit of measurement is kgm3\frac {\text{kg}} {\text{m}^3}.

ρ=mV \rho = \frac {m} {V}

Examples with Density

  1. The mass of the fuel in the tanker is 21,600 kg21,600\text{ kg}. We calculated the volume of the fuel, which is 29.7 m329.7\text{ m}^3. What is the density of the fuel?

ρ=mV=21,600 kg29.7 m3≈727 kg/m3 \rho = \frac{m}{V} = \frac{21,600\text{ kg}}{29.7\text{ m}^3} \approx 727\text{ kg/m}^3

  1. A beaker was filled with 500 g500\text{ g} of mercury. How high will the mercury stand in it if the inner radius of the beaker is 1.5 cm1.5\text{ cm}? The density of mercury is 13,600 kg/m313,600\text{ kg/m}^3.

V=mρ=0.5 kg13,600 kg/m3≈3.68⋅10−5 m3 V = \frac{m}{\rho} = \frac{0.5\text{ kg}}{13,600\text{ kg/m}^3} \approx 3.68 \cdot 10^{-5}\text{ m}^3

A=π⋅r2=π⋅(0.015 m)2≈7.07⋅10−4 m2 A = \pi \cdot r^2 = \pi \cdot (0.015\text{ m})^2 \approx 7.07 \cdot 10^{-4}\text{ m}^2

h=VA=3.68⋅10−5 m37.07⋅10−4 m2≈0.052 m=5.2 cm h = \frac{V}{A} = \frac{3.68 \cdot 10^{-5}\text{ m}^3}{7.07 \cdot 10^{-4}\text{ m}^2} \approx 0.052\text{ m} = 5.2\text{ cm}

  1. Calculate the mass of 1 cm31\text{ cm}^3 of mercury! How many kgm3\frac {\text{kg}} {\text{m}^3} is 1 gcm31\text{ }\frac {\text{g}} {\text{cm}^3}?

The density of mercury is 13,600 kgm313,600\text{ }\frac {\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}. Let us convert the density into units of gcm3\frac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3}:

ρ=13,600 kgm3=13,600⋅1000 g100 cm⋅100 cm⋅100 cm=13,600⋅1000 g1,000,000 cm3=13.6 gcm3 \rho = 13,600\text{ }\frac {\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3} = 13,600 \cdot \frac {1000\text{ g}} {100\text{ cm} \cdot 100\text{ cm} \cdot 100\text{ cm}} = 13,600 \cdot \frac{1000\text{ g}}{1,000,000\text{ cm}^3} = 13.6\text{ }\frac {\text{g}} {\text{cm}^3}

From this, the sought mass is:

m=ρ⋅V=13.6 gcm3⋅1 cm3=13.6 g m = \rho \cdot V = 13.6\text{ }\frac {\text{g}} {\text{cm}^3} \cdot 1\text{ cm}^3 = 13.6\text{ g}

From this it follows that 1 gcm31\text{ }\frac {\text{g}} {\text{cm}^3} is exactly equal to 1000 kgm31000\text{ }\frac {\text{kg}} {\text{m}^3}.
