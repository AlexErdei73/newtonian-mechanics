Exercises on Force II

Examples

  1. The barrel of a gun is 70.0 cm70.0\text{ cm} long, the mass of the bullet is 20.0 g20.0\text{ g}, and the muzzle velocity is 600 ms600\text{ }\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}. What is the acceleration of the bullet in the barrel? What force accelerates the bullet? How much time does it take for the bullet to accelerate in the barrel? Consider the acceleration to be uniform!

This problem might seem difficult at first glance, since neither the acceleration nor the time is directly known. However, we have two equations: one is used to calculate acceleration, and the other is for calculating the distance covered (v0=0v_0 = 0):

a=vt a = \frac {v} {t}

s=a2⋅t2 s = \frac {a} {2} \cdot t^2

Let’s convert the data into basic SI units (s=0.7 ms = 0.7\text{ m} and m=0.02 kgm = 0.02\text{ kg}), then substitute them into the formulas:

a=600t a = \frac {600} {t}

0.7=a2⋅t2 0.7 = \frac {a} {2} \cdot t^2

The system of equations can be easily rearranged algebraically:

a⋅t=600 a \cdot t = 600

1.4=(a⋅t)⋅t 1.4 = (a \cdot t) \cdot t

Substituting the value a⋅t=600a \cdot t = 600 obtained from the first equation into the second relation:

1.4=600⋅t 1.4 = 600 \cdot t

t=1.4600≈0.002333 s=2.333 ms t = \frac {1.4} {600} \approx 0.002333\text{ s} = 2.333\text{ ms}

Now, using the obtained time value, we can calculate the enormous acceleration of the bullet:

a=6000.002333≈257,143 ms2 a = \frac {600} {0.002333} \approx 257,143\text{ }\frac {\text{m}} {\text{s}^2}

Finally, based on Newton’s second law, the accelerating force is:

Fnet=m⋅a=0.020⋅257,143≈5143 N≈5.14 kN F_{\text{net}} = m \cdot a = 0.020 \cdot 257,143 \approx 5143\text{ N} \approx 5.14\text{ kN}

Thus, the bullet is accelerated in the gun barrel by an average force of 5.14 kN5.14\text{ kN} in just 2.33 ms2.33\text{ ms}.

  1. Upon hitting a wooden block, a bullet penetrates it at a speed of 600 ms600\text{ }\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} and comes to a complete stop over a distance of 30.0 cm30.0\text{ cm}. The mass of the bullet is still 20 g20\text{ g}. What is the deceleration, the duration of the braking, and the braking force exerted by the wooden block?

The equations of motion (by substituting v=0v = 0 and s=0.3 ms = 0.3\text{ m}):

a=v−v0t⟹a=−600t a = \frac {v - v_0} {t} \implies a = \frac {-600} {t}

s=v0⋅t+a2⋅t2⟹0.3=600⋅t+a2⋅t2 s = v_0 \cdot t + \frac {a} {2} \cdot t^2 \implies 0.3 = 600 \cdot t + \frac {a} {2} \cdot t^2

Let’s arrange the system of equations:

a⋅t=−600 a \cdot t = -600

0.6=1200⋅t+(a⋅t)⋅t 0.6 = 1200 \cdot t + (a \cdot t) \cdot t

Substitute the value a⋅t=−600a \cdot t = -600 into the second equation:

0.6=1200⋅t−600⋅t 0.6 = 1200 \cdot t - 600 \cdot t

0.6=600⋅t 0.6 = 600 \cdot t

t=0.6600=0.001 s=1.00 ms t = \frac{0.6}{600} = 0.001\text{ s} = 1.00\text{ ms}

The bullet decelerates in the wood in exactly 1 ms1\text{ ms}. Let’s calculate the deceleration:

a=−6000.001=−600,000 ms2 a = \frac {-600} {0.001} = -600,000\text{ }\frac {\text{m}} {\text{s}^2}

The negative sign indicates that the acceleration vector is in the opposite direction of the motion (deceleration). The magnitude of the braking force is:

|Fnet|=m⋅|a|=0.02⋅600,000=12,000 N=12.0 kN |F_{\text{net}}| = m \cdot |a| = 0.02 \cdot 600,000 = 12,000\text{ N} = 12.0\text{ kN}

The magnitude of the average braking force exerted by the wooden block is 12.0 kN12.0\text{ kN}.

  1. A fighter jet accelerates and takes off on a 600 m600\text{ m} long runway, with a liftoff speed of 280 kmh280\text{ }\frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}. If the mass of the jet is 17,000 kg17,000\text{ kg}, what thrust is required for takeoff? Consider the motion to have a constant acceleration!

At the very beginning of solving the problem, do not forget to convert the speed into SI units: v=2803.6≈77.78 msv = \frac{280}{3.6} \approx 77.78\text{ }\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}.

a=77.78t a = \frac {77.78} {t}

600=a2⋅t2 600 = \frac {a} {2} \cdot t^2

Rearranged:

a⋅t=77.78 a \cdot t = 77.78

1200=(a⋅t)⋅t⟹1200=77.78⋅t 1200 = (a \cdot t) \cdot t \implies 1200 = 77.78 \cdot t

t=120077.78≈15.43 s t = \frac {1200} {77.78} \approx 15.43\text{ s}

The value of uniform acceleration required for takeoff:

a=77.7815.43≈5.041 ms2 a = \frac {77.78} {15.43} \approx 5.041\text{ }\frac {\text{m}} {\text{s}^2}

According to Newton’s second law, the accelerating net thrust is:

Fnet=m⋅a=17,000⋅5.041≈85,697 N≈85.70 kN F_{\text{net}} = m \cdot a = 17,000 \cdot 5.041 \approx 85,697\text{ N} \approx 85.70\text{ kN}

Therefore, the magnitude of the useful thrust exerted by the engines is 85.70 kN85.70\text{ kN}.

2. Kicking a Football A player kicks an initially stationary football with a mass of 450 g450\text{ g} such that their foot is in contact with the ball for exactly 0.05 s0.05\text{ s}. The ball flies away at a speed of 30 ms30\text{ }\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} after the kick. What uniform acceleration did the ball gain during the contact, and what was the average force of the kick?

3. Launching an Arrow from a Bow An archer draws a bowstring over a length of 70 cm70\text{ cm}. Assume that the accelerating force is constant over the entire drawn length of the bow! With what acceleration does the arrow start, and what average force did the bow exert on it?
