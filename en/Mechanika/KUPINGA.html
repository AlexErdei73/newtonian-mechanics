Further Examples of Circular Motion *

The Conical Pendulum

Experiment

The Period

A conical pendulum is a pendulum that undergoes uniform circular motion in a horizontal plane, while the string traces out the surface of a cone. The body is acted upon by the vertically downward gravitational force and the tension force acting along the line of the string. The tension force can be decomposed into vertical and horizontal components. The vertical component of the tension balances the gravitational force, since the pendulum does not accelerate vertically. The circular motion takes place in a horizontal plane, meaning that the centripetal acceleration is horizontal and is produced by the horizontal component of the tension force.

Tcos⁡Θ=mg T \cos \Theta = mg

Tsin⁡Θ=macp T \sin \Theta = ma_{\text{cp}}

acp=v2R a_{\text{cp}} = \frac{v^2}{R}

We express TT from the first equation and substitute it into the second equation along with the centripetal acceleration:

T=mgcos⁡Θ T = \frac{mg}{\cos \Theta}

mgsin⁡Θcos⁡Θ=mv2R mg \frac{\sin \Theta}{\cos \Theta} = m \frac{v^2}{R}

v2=gRtan⁡Θ v^2 = gR \tan \Theta

Now we can use the relation:

v=Rω v = R\omega

(Rω)2=gRtan⁡Θ (R\omega)^2 = gR \tan \Theta

R2ω2=gRtan⁡Θ R^2 \omega^2 = gR \tan \Theta

ω2=gsin⁡ΘRcos⁡Θ \omega^2 = \frac{g \sin \Theta}{R \cos \Theta}

Next, we use:

R=lsin⁡Θ R = l \sin \Theta

This gives us:

ω2=gsin⁡Θlsin⁡Θcos⁡Θ \omega^2 = \frac{g \sin \Theta}{l \sin \Theta \cos \Theta}

2πT=glcos⁡Θ \frac{2\pi}{T} = \sqrt{\frac{g}{l \cos \Theta}}

T2π=lcos⁡Θg \frac{T}{2\pi} = \sqrt{\frac{l \cos \Theta}{g}}

T=2πlcos⁡Θg T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{l \cos \Theta}{g}}

This formula represents the period of a conical pendulum. If the angle Θ\Theta is very small compared to 1 rad1\text{ rad}, then the value of cos⁡Θ\cos \Theta can be taken as 11, and we recover the formula for the period of a simple pendulum swinging in a vertical plane at small displacements. We will discuss this later. We see, therefore, that the period of a conical pendulum for small cone angles is identical to the period of a simple pendulum at small displacements. This was also demonstrated in the video.

Example

A body of mass 0.1 kg0.1\text{ kg} undergoes uniform circular motion in a horizontal plane on a string of length 1 m1\text{ m}, while the angle between the string and the vertical is 30∘30^\circ. What is the radius of the circular path? What is the tension force stretching the string? What is the centripetal force? What is the velocity of the body? What is the period of revolution? Verify the answer using the formula obtained for the period as well!

R=lsin⁡Θ=1 m⋅sin⁡30∘=0.5 m R = l \sin \Theta = 1\text{ m} \cdot \sin 30^\circ = 0.5\text{ m}

Tcos⁡Θ=mg T \cos \Theta = mg

T=mgcos⁡Θ=0.1⋅9.81cos⁡30∘≈1.133 N T = \frac{mg}{\cos \Theta} = \frac{0.1 \cdot 9.81}{\cos 30^\circ} \approx 1.133\text{ N}

Fnet=Tsin⁡Θ=1.133⋅sin⁡30∘≈0.5663 N F_{\text{net}} = T \sin \Theta = 1.133 \cdot \sin 30^\circ \approx 0.5663\text{ N}

Fnet=ma F_{\text{net}} = ma

a=Fnetm=0.56630.1=5.663 m/s2 a = \frac{F_{\text{net}}}{m} = \frac{0.5663}{0.1} = 5.663\text{ m/s}^2

a=v2R a = \frac{v^2}{R}

5.663=v20.5 5.663 = \frac{v^2}{0.5}

2.8315=v2 2.8315 = v^2

v=2.8315≈1.683 m/s v = \sqrt{2.8315} \approx 1.683\text{ m/s}

v=2πRT v = \frac{2\pi R}{T}

T=2πRv=2π⋅0.51.683≈1.867 s T = \frac{2\pi R}{v} = \frac{2\pi \cdot 0.5}{1.683} \approx 1.867\text{ s}

Let us also calculate it using the formula we derived:

T=2πlcos⁡Θg=2π1⋅cos⁡30∘9,81≈1.867 s T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{l \cos \Theta}{g}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{1 \cdot \cos 30^\circ}{9,81}} \approx 1.867\text{ s}

Evidently, the formula yields exactly the same result as our step-by-step calculation, just as it should.

Race Car on a Banked Track

Example

A race car of mass 1200 kg1200\text{ kg} travels at a speed of 108 km/h108\text{ km/h} through a curve with a radius of 150 m150\text{ m}. The banking angle of the track relative to the horizontal is 30∘30^\circ. What are the normal constraint force and the static friction force exerted by the track? If μs=0.7\mu_{\text{s}} = 0.7, will the car slide out?

The speed of the car is low, so it would slide downwards along the banked track. This is prevented by a static friction force pointing upwards along the plane of the track.

We decompose the static friction force and the normal force into vertical components and horizontal components parallel to the acceleration. This yields:

Tcos⁡Θ+Fssin⁡Θ−mg=0 T \cos \Theta + F_{\text{s}} \sin \Theta - mg = 0

Tsin⁡Θ−Fscos⁡Θ=macp T \sin \Theta - F_{\text{s}} \cos \Theta = ma_{\text{cp}}

acp=v2R a_{\text{cp}} = \frac{v^2}{R}

108 km/h=108⋅1000 m3600 s=30 m/s 108\text{ km/h} = 108 \cdot \frac{1000\text{ m}}{3600\text{ s}} = 30\text{ m/s}

acp=v2R=302150=6 m/s2 a_{\text{cp}} = \frac{v^2}{R} = \frac{30^2}{150} = 6\text{ m/s}^2

Substituting the given values:

0.8660T+0.5Fs=11772 0.8660T + 0.5F_{\text{s}} = 11772

0.5T−0.8660Fs=7200 0.5T - 0.8660F_{\text{s}} = 7200

Expressing FsF_{\text{s}} from the first equation and substituting it into the second equation:

Fs=23544−1.732T F_{\text{s}} = 23544 - 1.732T

0.5T−0.8660(23544−1.732T)=7200 0.5T - 0.8660(23544 - 1.732T) = 7200

0.5T−20389.1+1.500T=7200 0.5T - 20389.1 + 1.500T = 7200

2T=27589.1 2T = 27589.1

T≈13794.6 N T \approx 13794.6\text{ N}

Fs=23544−1.732⋅13794.6≈−410.3 N F_{\text{s}} = 23544 - 1.732 \cdot 13794.6 \approx -410.3\text{ N}

Consequently, the sign of FsF_{\text{s}} is opposite to what we originally assumed, which means that in reality, it points downwards along the plane of the track. Without friction, the car would actually slide upwards and out of the track at this specific speed.
