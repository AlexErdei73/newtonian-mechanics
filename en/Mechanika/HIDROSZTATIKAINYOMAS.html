Hydrostatic Pressure

The Concept of Hydrostatic Pressure

Experiments

In the experiment, we can observe that a fluid exerts pressure due to its own weight. This pressure is called hydrostatic pressure.

The pressure originating from the weight of a fluid at rest is hydrostatic pressure.

Hydrostatic pressure increases with the height of the fluid column, but at any given depth, it is equal in all directions.

Hydrostatic pressure increases with the height of the fluid column. Hydrostatic pressure is also uniform in all directions at a given height.

Calculation of Hydrostatic Pressure

Example

Let us calculate the hydrostatic pressure at the bottom of a swimming pool. The base area of the pool is 10 m×5 m10\text{ m} \times 5\text{ m} and the water fills it to a height of 2 m2\text{ m}. The density of the water is 1000 kg/m31000\text{ kg/m}^3. * What is the base area of the pool? * What is the volume of water in the pool? * What is the mass of the water? * What is the weight of the water? * What is the hydrostatic pressure?

A=ab=10⋅5=50 m2 A = ab = 10 \cdot 5 = 50\text{ m}^2

V=Ah=50⋅2=100 m3 V = Ah = 50 \cdot 2 = 100\text{ m}^3

m=ρ⋅V=1000⋅100=100,000 kg m = \rho \cdot V = 1000 \cdot 100 = 100,000\text{ kg}

Fg=mg=100,000⋅9.81=981,000 N F_g = mg = 100,000 \cdot 9.81 = 981,000\text{ N}

p=FA=GA=981,00050=19,620 Pa p = \frac{F}{A} = \frac{G}{A} = \frac{981,000}{50} = 19,620\text{ Pa}

General derivation of pressure:

p=FA=mgA=ρVgA p = \frac{F}{A} = \frac{mg}{A} = \frac{\rho Vg}{A}

If the shape of the fluid column is a rectangular cuboid, the volume is:

V=Ah V = Ah

p=ρVgA=ρAhgA p = \frac{\rho Vg}{A} = \frac{\rho Ahg}{A}

p=ρgh p = \rho gh

Indeed, applying this formula to our example yields the correct value for hydrostatic pressure.

p=ρgh=1,000⋅9.81⋅2=19,620 Pa p = \rho gh = 1,000 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 2 = 19,620\text{ Pa}

Although we only derived this formula for a rectangular fluid column, it is clear that the pressure of a fluid at rest depends solely on the density of the fluid and the height of the fluid column, being directly proportional to both. Our result holds true for vessels and fluid columns of arbitrary shapes.

Examples

  1. What is the hydrostatic pressure of a mercury column that is 760 mm760\text{ mm} high? The density of mercury is 13,600 kg/m313,600\text{ kg/m}^3.

p=ρgh=13,600⋅9.81⋅0.76≈101,000 Pa p = \rho gh = 13,600 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.76 \approx 101,000\text{ Pa}

Thus, standard atmospheric pressure can balance out the pressure of a 76 cm76\text{ cm} high column of mercury.

  1. What height of a water column can be balanced out by standard atmospheric pressure? Let us take this value as 101,000 Pa101,000\text{ Pa}, rounded to three significant figures.

h=pρg=101,0001000⋅9.81=10.3 m h = \frac{p}{\rho g} = \frac{101,000}{1000 \cdot 9.81} = 10.3\text{ m}
