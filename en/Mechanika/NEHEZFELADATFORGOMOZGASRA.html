An Advanced Problem on Rotational Motion *

Example

A rod of mass mm and length LL is pivoted at its top and is capable of oscillating in a vertical plane. The rod, together with its pivot assembly, is able to rotate around a vertical axis passing through the pivot. A motor rotates the pivot assembly and maintains it at a constant angular velocity ω\omega. What angle Θ\Theta does the rod make with the vertical during its rotation if this angle remains constant?

The points of the rod perform circular motion in a horizontal plane, meaning their acceleration is the centripetal acceleration. The angle Θ\Theta does not change, meaning the rod has no angular acceleration in the coordinate system rotating along with it. Is there an angular acceleration in the inertial reference frame? If we resolve the acceleration of the points into components parallel and perpendicular to the rod, then for a non-zero angle Θ\Theta, there will be a component of acceleration perpendicular to the rod, which represents an angular acceleration relative to the horizontal axis of rotation.

ai,t=risin⁡Θω2cos⁡Θ a_{i,\text{t}} = r_i \sin \Theta\ \omega^2 \cos \Theta

α=ai,tri=ω2sin⁡Θcos⁡Θ \alpha = \frac{a_{i,\text{t}}}{r_i} = \omega^2 \sin \Theta \cos \Theta

Let us write the fundamental equation of rotational motion for the horizontal axis about which the rod can turn!

Mz,netext=Irodα M_{z,\text{net}}^{\text{ext}} = I_{\text{rod}} \alpha

Only the gravitational force exerts a torque:

Mz,netext=mgL2sin⁡Θ M_{z,\text{net}}^{\text{ext}} = mg \frac{L}{2} \sin \Theta

Substituting these expressions along with the moment of inertia of the rod relative to its endpoint, Irod=13mL2I_{\text{rod}} = \frac{1}{3}mL^2, we obtain the following equation:

mgL2sin⁡Θ=13mL2ω2sin⁡Θcos⁡Θ mg \frac{L}{2} \sin \Theta = \frac{1}{3} mL^2 \omega^2 \sin \Theta \cos \Theta

(g2−Lω23cosΘ)sin⁡Θ=0 \left( \frac{g}{2} - \frac{L\omega^2}{3}\cos \Theta \right)\sin \Theta = 0

This equation generally has two solutions.

Solution 1:

sin⁡Θ=0→Θ=0∘ \sin \Theta = 0 \quad \rightarrow \quad \Theta = 0^\circ

This is a stable solution for low angular velocities ω\omega. More precisely, it remains stable as long as the second solution does not exist.

Solution 2:

g2=Lω23cos⁡Θ \frac{g}{2} = \frac{L\omega^2}{3} \cos \Theta

From here, we can express the value of cos⁡Θ\cos \Theta:

cos⁡Θ=3g2Lω2 \cos \Theta = \frac{3g}{2L\omega^2}

Since the value of the cosine function cannot be greater than one:

cos⁡Θ≤1 \cos \Theta \leqslant 1

Thus, the angular velocity of the motor must be greater than a minimum critical value for this stable deflected solution to exist at all:

3g2Lω2≤1→ω≥3g2L \frac{3g}{2L\omega^2} \leqslant 1 \quad \rightarrow \quad \omega \geqslant \sqrt{\frac{3g}{2L}}

It can be shown that as soon as the motor’s speed crosses this critical limit, the vertical position becomes unstable, and the rod spontaneously deflects to the stable equilibrium angle Θ>0\Theta > 0.
