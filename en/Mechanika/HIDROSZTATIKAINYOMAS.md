# Hydrostatic Pressure

## The Concept of Hydrostatic Pressure

### Experiments

[Hydrostatic pressure and its dependence on the height of the fluid column](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DDFjFb72f94)

[Pascal's balance](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fodof4gSIA0&t=1188s)

In the experiment, we can observe that a fluid exerts pressure due to its own weight. This pressure is called hydrostatic pressure.

>**The pressure originating from the weight of a fluid at rest is hydrostatic pressure.**

Hydrostatic pressure increases with the height of the fluid column, but at any given depth, it is equal in all directions.

>**Hydrostatic pressure increases with the height of the fluid column. Hydrostatic pressure is also uniform in all directions at a given height.**

## Calculation of Hydrostatic Pressure

### Example
Let us calculate the hydrostatic pressure at the bottom of a swimming pool. The base area of the pool is $10\text{ m} \times 5\text{ m}$ and the water fills it to a height of $2\text{ m}$. The density of the water is $1000\text{ kg/m}^3$.
* What is the base area of the pool?
* What is the volume of water in the pool?
* What is the mass of the water?
* What is the weight of the water?
* What is the hydrostatic pressure?

$$
A = ab = 10 \cdot 5 = 50\text{ m}^2
$$

$$
V = Ah = 50 \cdot 2 = 100\text{ m}^3
$$

$$
m = \rho \cdot V = 1000 \cdot 100 = 100,000\text{ kg}
$$

$$
F_g = mg = 100,000 \cdot 9.81 = 981,000\text{ N}
$$

$$
p = \frac{F}{A} = \frac{G}{A} = \frac{981,000}{50} = 19,620\text{ Pa}
$$

General derivation of pressure:

$$
p = \frac{F}{A} = \frac{mg}{A} = \frac{\rho Vg}{A}
$$

If the shape of the fluid column is a rectangular cuboid, the volume is:

$$
V = Ah
$$

$$
p = \frac{\rho Vg}{A} = \frac{\rho Ahg}{A}
$$

$$
p = \rho gh
$$

Indeed, applying this formula to our example yields the correct value for hydrostatic pressure.

$$
p = \rho gh = 1,000 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 2 = 19,620\text{ Pa}
$$

Although we only derived this formula for a rectangular fluid column, it is clear that the pressure of a fluid at rest depends solely on the density of the fluid and the height of the fluid column, being directly proportional to both. Our result holds true for vessels and fluid columns of arbitrary shapes.

## Examples
1. What is the hydrostatic pressure of a mercury column that is $760\text{ mm}$ high? The density of mercury is $13,600\text{ kg/m}^3$.

$$
p = \rho gh = 13,600 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.76 \approx 101,000\text{ Pa}
$$

Thus, standard atmospheric pressure can balance out the pressure of a $76\text{ cm}$ high column of mercury.

2. What height of a water column can be balanced out by standard atmospheric pressure? Let us take this value as $101,000\text{ Pa}$, rounded to three significant figures.

$$
h = \frac{p}{\rho g} = \frac{101,000}{1000 \cdot 9.81} = 10.3\text{ m}
$$

## Problems

**Problem 1**
What is the hydrostatic pressure at the bottom of the world's deepest swimming pool, the $60\text{ m}$ deep Pearl Dive? The density of water is $1000\text{ kg/m}^3$, and the acceleration due to gravity is $g = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$.

**Problem 2**
A diver measures a hydrostatic pressure of $245,250\text{ Pa}$ during a dive. How deep is the diver in the freshwater lake if the density of water is $1000\text{ kg/m}^3$ and $g = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$?

**Problem 3**
An unknown liquid stands at a height of $50\text{ cm}$ in a laboratory beaker. The hydrostatic pressure at the bottom of the beaker is $3,924\text{ Pa}$. What is the density of the liquid? ($g = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$)
