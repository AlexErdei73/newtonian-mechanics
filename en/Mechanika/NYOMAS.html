Pressure

The Concept and Calculation of Pressure

Experiments

During the study of friction phenomena, we observed that the friction force also depended on the force compressing the contacting surfaces. However, the constraint force perpendicular to the surface, known as the normal force (or pressing force), can also cause deformation—for instance, leaving a footprint in flour or dough, or popping a balloon. In our experiments, we saw that the extent of deformation depends not only on the magnitude of the force but also on the size of the surface area over which this force acts. In physics, the deformation effect of a force can be characterized by introducing a new quantity: pressure.

Pressure is defined as the ratio of the normal force acting perpendicular to a surface to the area of that surface. The symbol for pressure is p, and its SI unit is the Pascal (Pa).

p=FA p = \frac{F}{A}

1 Pa=1 N1 m2 1\text{ Pa} = \frac{1\text{ N}}{1\text{ m}^2}

The role of the surface area is excellently illustrated by the bed of nails experiment. If we step onto a single nail, our entire body weight is concentrated onto a negligibly small surface area (AA), which generates an enormous pressure, causing the nail to instantly pierce right through the shoe. If, however, we lie down on a bed consisting of 500 nails, the pressing force (weight) is evenly distributed across a large surface area. Consequently, the pressure exerted on each individual nail is drastically reduced, leaving the balloon or the human body completely unharmed.

Examples

  1. A rectangular block with a mass of 200 g200\text{ g} has edges of 8 cm8\text{ cm}, 5 cm5\text{ cm}, and 2 cm2\text{ cm}. What pressure does the object exert at rest on a horizontal tabletop if the acceleration due to gravity is 9.81 m/s29.81\text{ m/s}^2?

F=Fg=mg=0.2⋅9.81=1.962 N F = F_g = mg = 0.2 \cdot 9.81 = 1.962\text{ N}

The three different contact surface areas (faces):

A1=ab=0.08⋅0.05=4⋅10−3 m2 A_1 = ab = 0.08 \cdot 0.05 = 4 \cdot 10^{-3}\text{ m}^2

A2=bc=0.05⋅0.02=10−3 m2 A_2 = bc = 0.05 \cdot 0.02 = 10^{-3}\text{ m}^2

A3=ac=0.08⋅0.02=1.6⋅10−3 m2 A_3 = ac = 0.08 \cdot 0.02 = 1.6 \cdot 10^{-3}\text{ m}^2

The pressures exerted in each respective case:

p1=FA1=1.9624⋅10−3=490.5 Pa p_1 = \frac{F}{A_1} = \frac{1.962}{4 \cdot 10^{-3}} = 490.5\text{ Pa}

p2=FA2=1.96210−3=1,962 Pa p_2 = \frac{F}{A_2} = \frac{1.962}{10^{-3}} = 1,962\text{ Pa}

p3=FA3=1.9621.6⋅10−3=1,226.25 Pa p_3 = \frac{F}{A_3} = \frac{1.962}{1.6 \cdot 10^{-3}} = 1,226.25\text{ Pa}

It can be seen that the block leaves the deepest impression when contacting the surface with its smallest area face (A2A_2), because it exerts the greatest pressure in this configuration.

  1. A balloon is pressed onto the tip of a nail with a diameter of 0.1 mm0.1\text{ mm} with just enough force to make it burst. At this moment, the pressure is 38.20 MPa38.20\text{ MPa}. What force is exerted at the instant of bursting? What will the pressure be if the balloon is pressed with the exact same force onto a bed consisting of 100 identical nails?

p=FA p = \frac{F}{A}

The circular cross-sectional area of a single nail tip (d=0.1 mm→R=0.05 mm=0.05⋅10−3 md = 0.1\text{ mm} \rightarrow R = 0.05\text{ mm} = 0.05 \cdot 10^{-3}\text{ m}):

A=R2π=(0.05⋅10−3)2⋅3.14159≈7.854⋅10−9 m2 A = R^2 \pi = (0.05 \cdot 10^{-3})^2 \cdot 3.14159 \approx 7.854 \cdot 10^{-9}\text{ m}^2

The exerted force:

F=pA=38.20⋅106⋅7.854⋅10−9≈0.300 N F = pA = 38.20 \cdot 10^6 \cdot 7.854 \cdot 10^{-9} \approx 0.300\text{ N}

If we press the balloon onto 100 identical nails, the surface area increases a hundredfold (Anew=100AA_{\text{new}} = 100A), so the pressure drops to one-hundredth of its original value:

pnew=F100A=38.20⋅106100=382,000 Pa=382 kPa p_{\text{new}} = \frac{F}{100A} = \frac{38.20 \cdot 10^6}{100} = 382,000\text{ Pa} = 382\text{ kPa}

Atmospheric Pressure

Experiments

These experiments demonstrate that the surrounding air continuously exerts an extremely high pressure on its environment. This pressure remains virtually unnoticed as long as the internal pressure of objects balances it out. However, as soon as this equilibrium is disrupted—either because we evacuate the air from inside the hemispheres, or because heated water vapor rapidly condenses upon cooling, leading to a dramatic drop in internal pressure—the immense external atmospheric pressure and the resulting net force can crush steel drums or aluminum cans with brutal force.

The standard atmospheric pressure value is 101,325 Pa=1 atm101,325\text{ Pa} = 1\text{ atm}. Another unit similar to the atmosphere and frequently used in engineering is the bar: 1 bar=105 Pa1\text{ bar} = 10^5\text{ Pa}. The nominal value of atmospheric pressure is referenced to sea level. Atmospheric pressure decreases rapidly with altitude, since the thickness of the air layer above us that weighs down on us becomes increasingly thinner. Weather fronts also significantly influence the local value of atmospheric pressure.

Example

  1. How many N\text{N} of force are required to pull apart the Magdeburg hemispheres of radius 5 cm5\text{ cm} if the air inside them has been completely evacuated? Let the external atmospheric pressure be exactly 1.01⋅105 Pa1.01 \cdot 10^5\text{ Pa}!

Atmospheric pressure acts perpendicularly from all directions onto the spherical surface, but when calculating the force needed to pull them apart, the relevant area is the cross-sectional plane perpendicular to the direction of pulling (the area of the circular disk where they mate):

A=R2π=0.052⋅3.14159≈7.854⋅10−3 m2 A = R^2 \pi = 0.05^2 \cdot 3.14159 \approx 7.854 \cdot 10^{-3}\text{ m}^2

The force compressing the hemispheres together:

F=pA=1.01⋅105⋅7.854⋅10−3≈793.25 N F = pA = 1.01 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 7.854 \cdot 10^{-3} \approx 793.25\text{ N}

This force corresponds to the weight of a mass of approximately 81 kg81\text{ kg}, which explains why it is impossible to pull them apart with bare hands without breaking the vacuum.
