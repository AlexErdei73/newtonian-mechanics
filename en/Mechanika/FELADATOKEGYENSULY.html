Exercises on Static Equilibrium *

Examples

  1. On a horizontal ceiling, two hooks are located at a distance of 3.00 m3.00\text{ m} from each other. A rope is attached to them such that an object with a mass of 0.10 kg0.10\text{ kg} hangs from the middle of the rope. The object is located exactly at the midpoint of the rope, so the segments of the rope are of equal length, and the object is vertically 2.00 m2.00\text{ m} below the level of the ceiling. What is the length of the rope? What is the tensile force generated in the rope when the object is in equilibrium? The acceleration due to gravity is g=9.81 ms2g = 9.81\text{ }\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}, but the simulation uses the rounded value of g=9.80 ms2g = 9.80\text{ }\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}, so we will work with this value in the calculation.

Simulation

Statics first example interactive simulation

The layout of the example and the simulation results are shown in the figures below regarding the object and one of the rope segments:

Let’s perform the calculations! The rope segments are of equal length, and due to geometric symmetry, the tensile force generated in them is also equal in magnitude; let’s denote this simply by TT. Let the horizontal axis pointing to the right be the xx-axis of the coordinate system, and let the vertical axis pointing upward be the yy-axis. Based on the right-angled triangle, the length of one rope segment (ll) is:

l=1.52+22=2.25+4=6.25=2.50 m l = \sqrt {1.5^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{2.25 + 4} = \sqrt{6.25} = 2.50\text{ m}

Thus, the total length of the rope (2l2l) is exactly 5.00 m5.00\text{ m}. Let α\alpha denote the angle enclosed by the rope segment with the vertical. Then the values of the trigonometric functions are:

sin⁡α=1.52.5=0.6 \sin \alpha = \frac {1.5} {2.5} = 0.6

cos⁡α=22.5=0.8 \cos \alpha = \frac {2} {2.5} = 0.8

The condition for the static equilibrium of the object is that the net force acting on it must be a zero vector:

T→1+T→2+F→g=0→ \vec{T}_1 + \vec{T}_2 + \vec{F}_g = \vec{0}

Here, index 11 refers to the left rope segment and index 22 refers to the right rope segment. Due to symmetry, the magnitudes of the forces are identical:

|T→1|=|T→2|=T |\vec{T}_1| = |\vec{T}_2| = T

Let’s write the vector equation according to the axial coordinates:

T1x+T2x+Fg,x=0 T_{1x} + T_{2x} + F_{g,x} = 0

T1y+T2y+Fg,y=0 T_{1y} + T_{2y} + F_{g,y} = 0

Based on the geometric resolution, the values of the components are:

T1x=−T⋅sin⁡α T_{1x} = -T \cdot \sin \alpha

T2x=T⋅sin⁡α T_{2x} = T \cdot \sin \alpha

T1y=T2y=T⋅cos⁡α T_{1y} = T_{2y} = T \cdot \cos \alpha

Fg,x=0,Fg,y=−Fg=−m⋅g=−0.10⋅9.80=−0.98 N F_{g,x} = 0,\ \ F_{g,y} = -F_g = -m \cdot g = -0.10 \cdot 9.80 = -0.98\text{ N}

Substituting these components into the equations:

−T⋅sin⁡α+T⋅sin⁡α+0=0 -T \cdot \sin \alpha + T \cdot \sin \alpha + 0 = 0

T⋅cos⁡α+T⋅cos⁡α−Fg=0 T \cdot \cos \alpha + T \cdot \cos \alpha - F_g = 0

The first (horizontal) equation is a trivial identity, and from the second (vertical) equation:

2T⋅cos⁡α=Fg 2T \cdot \cos \alpha = F_g

T=Fg2⋅cos⁡α=0.98 N2⋅0.8=0.981.6=0.6125 N T = \frac {F_g} {2 \cdot \cos \alpha} = \frac {0.98\text{ N}} {2 \cdot 0.8} = \frac{0.98}{1.6} = 0.6125\text{ N}

Rounded to three significant figures, the tensile force generated in the rope is exactly 0.613 N0.613\text{ N}, which perfectly matches the value measured in the simulator.

  1. An object with a mass of 0.10 kg0.10\text{ kg} is attached to a horizontal ceiling with a rope. Using another rope, the object is pulled horizontally to the right such that it moves exactly 1.50 m1.50\text{ m} to the right from the vertical line of the suspension point, coming to equilibrium at a depth of 2.00 m2.00\text{ m} below the level of the ceiling. How long is the inclined rope attaching the object to the ceiling? What forces stretch the ropes in this state of equilibrium? (The acceleration here is also g=9.80 ms2g = 9.80\text{ }\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}).

Simulation

Statics second example interactive simulation

The geometric arrangement and the measured results are shown in the figures below:

The length of the left (inclined) rope is equal to the length calculated in the first example, since the legs of the triangle are exactly the same. Thus, we get 2.50 m2.50\text{ m} for the length of the rope. Consequently, the values of the trigonometric functions with the vertical are again: sin⁡α=0.6\sin \alpha = 0.6 and cos⁡α=0.8\cos \alpha = 0.8 (where α\alpha is the angle enclosed by the left rope with the vertical).

The vector equilibrium equation:

T→1+T→2+F→g=0→ \vec{T}_1 + \vec{T}_2 + \vec{F}_g = \vec{0}

The system of equations for the coordinate components:

T1x+T2x+Fg,x=0 T_{1x} + T_{2x} + F_{g,x} = 0

T1y+T2y+Fg,y=0 T_{1y} + T_{2y} + F_{g,y} = 0

Based on the direction of the forces, the values of the coordinates are:

T1x=−T1⋅sin⁡α T_{1x} = -T_1 \cdot \sin \alpha

T1y=T1⋅cos⁡α T_{1y} = T_1 \cdot \cos \alpha

T2x=T2,T2y=0 T_{2x} = T_2,\ \ T_{2y} = 0

Fg,x=0,Fg,y=−Fg=−0.98 N F_{g,x} = 0,\ \ F_{g,y} = -F_g = -0.98\text{ N}

Substituting these components into the system of equations:

−T1⋅sin⁡α+T2+0=0 -T_1 \cdot \sin \alpha + T_2 + 0 = 0

T1⋅cos⁡α+0−Fg=0 T_1 \cdot \cos \alpha + 0 - F_g = 0

From the second (vertical) equation, the force T1T_1 generated in the inclined rope can be directly expressed:

T1=Fgcos⁡α=0.98 N0.8=1.225 N T_1 = \frac {F_g} {\cos \alpha} = \frac {0.98\text{ N}} {0.8} = 1.225\text{ N}

Substituting this value back into the first (horizontal) equation, we get the magnitude of the horizontal pulling force:

T2=T1⋅sin⁡α=1.225 N⋅0.6=0.735 N T_2 = T_1 \cdot \sin \alpha = 1.225\text{ N} \cdot 0.6 = 0.735\text{ N}

Thus, the left inclined rope is stretched with a force of 1.23 N1.23\text{ N}, while the tensile force generated in the right horizontal tension rope is 0.735 N0.735\text{ N}.
