# Cavendish's Experiment

## Significance of the Measurement

The gravitational constant $G$ present in the law of gravitation was a completely unknown quantity in Newton's time. Its measurement was achieved by Henry Cavendish approximately 100 years after Newton's death. It cannot be determined solely from the orbital data of bodies orbiting the Earth because in those equations it always appears multiplied by the mass of the Earth. However, once this constant is measured, we can immediately calculate the mass of the Earth or any other celestial body, including the Sun. Cavendish thus referred to his measurement as "weighing the Earth."

The challenge is that the gravitational attraction between normal-sized objects represents an extremely small force. Measuring such a tiny force is very difficult. Even today, our measurements of $G$ are only accurate to about 4 significant figures, even when using the most sophisticated methods.

Cavendish used a device called a torsion balance, which consists of a dumbbell-shaped body suspended by a thin torsion wire. Two spheres of equal mass and size are located at the ends of the dumbbell, which is suspended at its center. The structure must be isolated as much as possible from all disturbing external influences. If two large lead spheres are placed near the two smaller spheres so that their gravitational attraction twists the torsion wire, the angle of twist can be measured. The gravitational constant can then be calculated using the experimental masses, distances, and the properties of the torsion wire.

## Examples

1. Determine the mass of the Earth, knowing that the acceleration due to gravity is $9.81\text{ m/s}^2$ and the radius of the Earth is $6370\text{ km}$! The measured value of $G$ is $6.674 \cdot 10^{-11}\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2\text{/kg}^2$.

$$
F_{\text{g}} = mg
$$

$$
F_{\text{g}} = G\frac{mM_{\text{E}}}{R^2}
$$

Here, we have written the gravitational force acting on the body in two different forms! One form is the standard one containing the acceleration due to gravity. The other represents Newton's law of gravitation applied to this problem.

$$
mg = G\frac{mM_{\text{E}}}{R^2}
$$

$$
g = \frac{GM_{\text{E}}}{R^2}
$$

From this, we can express the value of $M_{\text{E}}$, the mass of the Earth:

$$
M_{\text{E}} = \frac{gR^2}{G} = \frac{9.81 \cdot 6\,370\,000^2}{6.674 \cdot 10^{-11}} \approx 5.964 \cdot 10^{24}\text{ kg}
$$

2. Determine the mass of the Sun based on the Earth's orbital data!

$$
F_{\text{g}} = m\frac{v^2}{r} = m\frac{4\pi^2 r^2}{T^2 r}
$$

$$
F_{\text{g}} = G\frac{mM_{\text{S}}}{r^2}
$$

Equating the two expressions gives the following:

$$
m\frac{4\pi^2 r}{T^2} = G\frac{mM_{\text{S}}}{r^2}
$$

Here we can divide by $m$ and express the value of $M_{\text{S}}$:

$$
M_{\text{S}} = \frac{4\pi^2 r^3}{GT^2} = \frac{4 \cdot 3.1415^2 \cdot (1.496 \cdot 10^{11})^3}{6.674 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot (365.26 \cdot 86\,400)^2} \approx 1.988 \cdot 10^{30}\text{ kg}
$$

## Exercises

1. In Example 1, we calculated the mass of the Earth ($M_{\text{E}} \approx 5.964 \cdot 10^{24}\text{ kg}$). Consider the Earth to be a solid sphere with a radius of $R = 6370\text{ km}$. Calculate the average density of the Earth!

2. Two ocean liners, each with a mass of $50\,000\text{ tonnes}$, are parked next to each other in a harbor. The distance between their centers of mass is $100\text{ m}$. How large is the gravitational attractive force acting between them? Compare this force to the weight of a $10\text{ dkg}$ apple! ($G = 6.674 \cdot 10^{-11}\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2\text{/kg}^2$, $g = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$)

3. The mass of the Moon is approximately 1/81 of the Earth's mass ($M_{\text{M}} = 7.35 \cdot 10^{22}\text{ kg}$), and its radius is approximately $1737\text{ km}$. What is the acceleration due to gravity on the surface of the Moon? If an astronaut along with their gear weighs $120\text{ kg}$ on Earth, what is their weight on the Moon?
