Examples of Elastic Collisions

Rebound of Two Stacked Balls

Experiment

Simulation

Rebound of two stacked balls

Let the lower ball be 10 times more massive than the upper ball. Calculate the rebound velocity of the upper ball and determine its maximum height as well! Let the initial height be h0h_0!

11mgh0=11mv022 11mgh_0 = \frac{11mv_0^2}{2}

2gh0=v02 2gh_0 = v_0^2

Both balls reach the ground with this velocity v0v_0. First, the lower ball rebounds with a velocity of magnitude v0v_0. At this moment, it collides with the second (upper) ball, which arrives with the same speed but in the opposite direction. Suppose that the collision is central and perfectly elastic!

9mv0=10mv1+mv2 9mv_0 = 10mv_1 + mv_2

10mv022+mv022=10mv122+mv222 \frac{10mv_0^2}{2} + \frac{mv_0^2}{2} = \frac{10mv_1^2}{2} + \frac{mv_2^2}{2}

From the first equation:

v2=9v0−10v1 v_2 = 9v_0 - 10v_1

The simplified form of the second equation:

11v02=10v12+v22 11v_0^2 = 10v_1^2 + v_2^2

Substituting the expression for v2v_2 into this:

11v02=10v12+(9v0−10v1)2 11v_0^2 = 10v_1^2 + (9v_0 - 10v_1)^2

11v02=10v12+81v02−180v0v1+100v12 11v_0^2 = 10v_1^2 + 81v_0^2 - 180v_0 v_1 + 100v_1^2

We obtain the following parametric quadratic equation for v1v_1:

0=110v12−180v0v1+70v02 0 = 110v_1^2 - 180v_0 v_1 + 70v_0^2

0=11v12−18v0v1+7v02 0 = 11v_1^2 - 18v_0 v_1 + 7v_0^2

v1=18v0±324v02−4⋅11⋅7v022⋅11=18v0±4v022=v0or711v0 v_1 = \frac{18v_0 \pm \sqrt{324v_0^2 - 4 \cdot 11 \cdot 7v_0^2}}{2 \cdot 11} = \frac{18v_0 \pm 4v_0}{22} = v_0 \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{7}{11}v_0

We are interested in the second solution, which we substitute into the equation used to calculate v2v_2:

v2=9v0−10⋅7v011=2911v0≈2.636v0 v_2 = 9v_0 - 10 \cdot \frac{7v_0}{11} = \frac{29}{11}v_0 \approx 2.636 v_0

Now that we have obtained v2v_2, which is the rebound velocity of the upper ball, we can easily calculate the final height of the ball:

mgh=m(2.636v0)22 mgh = \frac{m(2.636 v_0)^2}{2}

h=6.950v022g≈6.950h0 h = \frac{6.950 v_0^2}{2g} \approx 6.950 h_0

Thus, the upper ball rebounds to nearly 6.95 times the release height!

Newton’s Cradle

Experiment

Simulation

Newton’s cradle simulation

The operation of the toy can be understood most simply by assuming perfectly elastic collisions. In this case, both momentum and mechanical energy are conserved. Suppose we lift two balls and release them on one side. Then the initial energy is:

Em,0=2mv022=mv02 E_{\text{m},0} = \frac{2mv_0^2}{2} = mv_0^2

The initial momentum:

p0=2mv0 p_0 = 2mv_0

Exactly the same energy and momentum must appear at the other end of the system for both conservation laws to be satisfied. Is it possible for only one ball to rise?

Due to the conservation of momentum:

p0=p p_0 = p

2mv0=mv 2mv_0 = mv

v=2v0 v = 2v_0

The velocity of the single rising ball would be twice as large as the velocity with which the two balls collided. The energy would then be:

Em=mv22=4mv022=2mv02 E_{\text{m}} = \frac{mv^2}{2} = \frac{4mv_0^2}{2} = 2mv_0^2

This is not possible because the energy would double:

Em=2Em,0 E_{\text{m}} = 2E_{\text{m},0}

Is it possible for three balls to rise? In this case, the velocity would be:

2mv0=3mv 2mv_0 = 3mv

v=23v0≈0.6667v0 v = \frac{2}{3}v_0 \approx 0.6667 v_0

The mechanical energy would then be:

Em=3m(0.6667v0)22≈0.6667mv02=0.6667Em,0 E_{\text{m}} = \frac{3m(0.6667 v_0)^2}{2} \approx 0.6667 mv_0^2 = 0.6667 E_{\text{m},0}

If three balls were to swing out, one-third of the energy would be lost, so this is not possible either. Therefore, exactly the same number of balls will swing out on the far side as we originally swung out.

There is another very important factor that is rarely mentioned when explaining the operation of the toy: the balls must be completely identical, primarily having equal masses. We have seen that this is the condition for a stationary ball to completely absorb the energy and momentum of the moving ball during a central, perfectly elastic collision. It can easily be tested using the simulation that the motion of the toy is disrupted if the mass of any ball differs from the others. In such cases, the system loses the beautiful, regular rhythm we are used to.

Exercises

  1. Two stacked balls are dropped from a height of h0h_0. The mass of the lower ball is MM, and the mass of the upper ball is mm. The collisions with the ground and with each other are perfectly elastic and central.

    • Show that the rebound velocity of the upper ball is:

    vupper=3M−mM+mv0v_{\text{upper}} = \frac{3M - m}{M + m} v_0

    where

    v0=2gh0v_0 = \sqrt{2gh_0}

    • Determine the height to which the upper ball rebounds!
    • Examine the limiting case where M≫mM \gg m! What do you observe?

  2. In a Newton’s cradle, there are 5 identical balls. Two are raised to a height of h0h_0 on one side and released.

    • Show, using the laws of conservation of momentum and energy, that exactly two balls will swing out on the other side!
    • At what velocity do they start?
    • What would happen if the mass of the middle ball were twice as large as the others? Would the system work the same way? Justify your answer!
