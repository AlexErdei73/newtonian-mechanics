Exercises on Accelerated Motion

Examples of Accelerated Motion with Zero Initial Velocity

  1. A car accelerates from rest to 100 km/h100\text{ km/h} in 8.00 s8.00\text{ s}. What is the final velocity in m/s\text{m/s}? What is the acceleration? What is the distance traveled during acceleration? The acceleration can be considered uniform throughout the process.

v=st=100 km1 h=100,000 m3600 s≈27.8 ms v = \frac {s} {t} = \frac {100\text{ km}} {1\text{ h}} = \frac {100,000\text{ m}} {3600\text{ s}} \approx 27.8\text{ }\frac {\text{m}} {\text{s}}

Here, we used the fundamental concept of uniform motion to convert the final velocity, as we can assume the car would continue to travel at this constant speed after the acceleration phase. This means it would cover a distance of 100 km100\text{ km} in 1 h1\text{ h}. We know that 1 hour=60 minutes=3600 seconds1\text{ hour} = 60\text{ minutes} = 3600\text{ seconds}, since each minute contains 60 s60\text{ s}.

a=Δvt=v−v0t=27.8 ms−08 s≈3.48 ms2 a = \frac {\Delta v} {t} = \frac {v - v_0} {t} = \frac {27.8\text{ }\frac {\text{m}} {\text{s}} - 0} {8\text{ s}} \approx 3.48\text{ }\frac {\text{m}} {\text{s}^2}

s=a2⋅t2=3.48 ms22⋅(8 s)2=111 m s = \frac {a} {2} \cdot t^2 = \frac {3.48\text{ }\frac {\text{m}} {\text{s}^2}} {2} \cdot (8\text{ s})^2 = 111\text{ m}

The results are rounded to three significant figures, as required by the precision of the initial data, so the final answer should not suggest greater precision.

  1. An object slides down a 30∘30^\circ inclined plane without friction, starting from rest, with an acceleration of 5.00 ms25.00\text{ }\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}. If the length of the incline is 10.0 m10.0\text{ m}, how many seconds does it take to reach the bottom of the incline? At what velocity does it arrive?

s=a2⋅t2 s = \frac {a} {2} \cdot t^2

Substituting the known data, where the unknown time is denoted by xx (units are omitted during the calculation, and the result is understood to be in seconds):

10.0=5.002⋅x2 10.0 = \frac {5.00} {2} \cdot x^2

10.0=2.50⋅x2 10.0 = 2.50 \cdot x^2

4.00=x2 4.00 = x^2

x=±2.00 x = \pm 2.00

Since the elapsed time can physically only be a positive value, the correct solution is t=2.00 st = 2.00\text{ s}. The precision is reported here rounded to three significant figures as well.

Calculating the final velocity from the acceleration formula:

a=v−v0t a = \frac {v - v_0} {t}

5.00=x−02.00 5.00 = \frac {x - 0} {2.00}

x=10.0 x = 10.0

Thus, the object reaches a velocity of exactly 10.0 ms10.0\text{ }\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} at the bottom of the incline.

Examples of Accelerated Motion with Non-Zero Initial Velocity

  1. The deceleration of a ball thrown vertically upward due to the gravitational force is 9.81 ms29.81\text{ }\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}. How high does it fly if it is launched with an initial velocity of 5.00 ms5.00\text{ }\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}, and how much time does it take to reach the peak of its trajectory?

If we apply the standard formula, the value of acceleration must be taken into account with a negative sign, since the velocity of the ball continuously decreases (decelerates). At the highest point, its instantaneous velocity becomes zero (v=0v = 0).

a=v−v0t a = \frac {v - v_0} {t}

−9.81=0−5.00x -9.81 = \frac {0 - 5.00} {x}

−9.81⋅x=−5.00 -9.81 \cdot x = -5.00

x=−5.00−9.81≈0.510 x = \frac {-5.00} {-9.81} \approx 0.510

Thus, the duration of the ascent is t=0.510 st = 0.510\text{ s}.

The maximum height is determined using the quadratic law of motion:

s=v0⋅t+a2⋅t2=5.00⋅0.510+−9.812⋅0.5102≈1.27 m s = v_0 \cdot t + \frac {a} {2} \cdot t^2 = 5.00 \cdot 0.510 + \frac {-9.81} {2} \cdot 0.510^2 \approx 1.27\text{ m}

The ball flies to a maximum height of 1.27 m1.27\text{ m}.

  1. A car brakes from an initial velocity of 30.0 ms30.0\text{ }\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} with a constant deceleration of 3.00 ms23.00\text{ }\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}, covering a distance of 113 m113\text{ m} during the braking distance. How long did the braking process last, and to what velocity did the vehicle slow down?

This task might seem complex at first, but notice that in the quadratic law of motion, all parameters are given except for time. The time duration can therefore be uniquely determined by solving a quadratic equation.

s=v0⋅t+a2⋅t2 s = v_0 \cdot t + \frac {a} {2} \cdot t^2

113=30.0⋅x+−3.002⋅x2 113 = 30.0 \cdot x + \frac {-3.00} {2} \cdot x^2

Rearranging the equation to equal zero:

1.50⋅x2−30.0⋅x+113=0 1.50 \cdot x^2 - 30.0 \cdot x + 113 = 0

To determine the time xx, we apply the well-known quadratic formula:

x1,2=−b±b2−4ac2a=30.0±30.02−4⋅1.50⋅1132⋅1.50=30.0±900−6783.00=30.0±14.93.00 x_{1,2} = \frac {-b \pm \sqrt {b^2 - 4ac}} {2a} = \frac {30.0 \pm \sqrt {30.0^2 - 4 \cdot 1.50 \cdot 113}} {2 \cdot 1.50} = \frac {30.0 \pm \sqrt {900 - 678}} {3.00} = \frac {30.0 \pm 14.9} {3.00}

At the end of the calculation, we obtain two mathematical solutions: x1=15.0x_1 = 15.0 and x2=5.03x_2 = 5.03.

Physically, the two values carry the following meaning: in the first case (5.03 s5.03\text{ s}), the car traveling straight reaches the specified distance without changing direction. The larger time value (15.0 s15.0\text{ s}) would manifest if the braking acceleration continued to act continuously even after the vehicle came to a complete stop: the car would slow down to zero, turn around, and accelerate backward to reach the 113 m113\text{ m} distance measured from the starting point once again. Since in reality braking ends upon stopping or when the desired velocity is reached, only the shorter time before the direction of motion reverses is relevant to us. The deceleration time is therefore t=5.03 st = 5.03\text{ s}.

The velocity reached can then be calculated simply from the fundamental acceleration formula:

a=v−v0t a = \frac {v - v_0} {t}

−3.00=x−30.05.03 -3.00 = \frac {x - 30.0} {5.03}

−15.1=x−30.0 -15.1 = x - 30.0

x=14.9 x = 14.9

The velocity of the car at the end of the segment is 14.9 ms14.9\text{ }\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}.
