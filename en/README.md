### Required Mathematical Prerequisites

The mathematics in this course are deliberately maintained at a high school level. This means we do not utilize differential and integral calculus, nor do we introduce differential equations. You will, however, need a solid and confident grasp of the following mathematical topics:

*   **Number Systems:** A thorough understanding of real numbers, including the properties and operations governing natural numbers, integers, and rational numbers.
*   **Algebra:** Absolute proficiency in solving linear, quadratic, and radical equations, as well as systems of linear equations. Exponents, powers, and logarithms will also emerge contextually. We will use the standard sigma ($\sum$) notation for compact representations of sums.
*   **Geometry:** Clear understanding of angles — with special emphasis on angles with parallel and perpendicular sides — and the Pythagorean theorem. Basic proficiency in calculating the perimeter, area, surface area, and volume of geometric shapes and solid bodies is expected.
*   **Trigonometry:** Confident mastery of right-triangle trigonometry and trigonometric functions for arbitrary angles. Measuring angles in radians and applying trigonometric addition formulas are highly essential.

This foundational mathematical framework will be summarized on a separate website in the future, should this project garner wider interest.

### How to Use These Lectures

My goal is to teach you the deep inner logic of the material rather than presenting a dry directory of equations. You must learn the fundamental laws thoroughly; these are explicitly locked in highlighted definitions. Beyond these core pillars, it is completely unnecessary to memorize or retain every single derived equation.

As you browse the course chapters, you will encounter several lessons marked with an asterisk (`*`). Comprehending these advanced tracks may require trigonometry, or they may simply present a higher conceptual difficulty. You may safely skip them during your first pass, but I highly recommend reinforcing your mathematical background and returning to study these advanced lessons later on.

It is absolutely paramount to work through the solved lecture examples with pen and paper in hand, and to solve the seminar exercises independently. Always try to verify your final answers using dimensional analysis (unit cross-checking), the interactive simulations, or rough orders-of-magnitude estimations. Most calculation errors can be filtered out instantly this way.

Never report a final numerical result with a higher precision than the least accurate given value in the problem statement! To effectively minimize rounding discrepancies, your final value should report a maximum of one additional decimal place beyond the initial data points.
